As per my analysis, the top 3 linked pages that were most similar to the Chicken page were:
rank    title               doc_id  cosine_similarity
1       Laying_hen          18      0.5030
2       Chicken_as_food     1       0.4558
3       Red_junglefowl      9       0.3655

These 3 documents are topically close to Chicekn but for different reasons.

"Laying_hen" is actually not a single page but rather a heading in the page "Poultry Farming". 
This has a definite impact on the cosine similarity because the number of tokens is far fewer than 
that of a full article. This means there are less unique terms and lower frequency terms in 
"Laying Hen" than in any other link, making it so there are fewer terms available to match 
against another document. This would in turn make the overlap of words such as "hen" and "egg"
to look very strong. The TF-IDF in this case is less representative of its true topic and the cosine
similarity is potentially misleading. 

Next is "Chicken_as_food" which is similar to Chicken because they share the same vocabulary 
such as "meat", "breed", or "egg". 

Red_junglefowl was the next most similar to Chicken and this is because it is the page dedicated to 
the direct wild ancestor of the domesticated chicken and therfore shares the same biological vocabulary 
like "species", "breed", and "feather".

Surprisingly, lemmatization did not have a noticeable impact on my results. The heatmap remained moderately 
the same except that the colors seemed less bright, meaning that the similarities shifted slightly but the 
distribution remained the same. This could be because lemmatization may not have much of an impact on high-weight
distinctive terms that affect the similarity rankings the most. It may only have an impact on terms that 
can merge to the same root but have different meaning, making two documents appear slightly more similar than 
what they actually are. But these terms make the minority and thus do not have a big weight on the similarity rankings.
